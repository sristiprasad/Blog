# Market Research and Analysis: How Rapido Uses Consumer Behaviour to Build a Better Ride Experience

I garner my understanding of Rapido, through leveraging insightful reports and research published by researchers and the company itself.

A study titled *A Study on Customer Satisfaction Towards Rapido Bike Taxi Services in Erode City* offers an interesting look into how customers perceive the platform and where there is room to improve.

## What does customer behaviour tell us?

The study surveyed 50 respondents in Erode City, Tamil Nadu to understand their preferences, satisfaction levels and expectations.

A few findings stood out:

- **38%** primarily associated Rapido with quick service.
- **34%** chose it for its low cost.
- **46%** used Rapido for emergency travel.
- **62%** felt that Rapido helped save travel time.
- **60%** described their ride experience as comfortable.
- **40%** preferred UPI as their payment method.

These numbers tell us something interesting: customers aren't just buying a ride. They are buying saved time, affordability and convenience.

For a product team, this is useful information. It helps identify which parts of the experience are working and which deserve more attention.

## Turning customer insights into product improvements

Customer research becomes meaningful when it translates into better product decisions.

Here are a few opportunities that emerge from the study:

**1. Make pricing more predictable**

Affordability is one of Rapido's biggest attractions. However, price fluctuations can affect customer perception.

A more transparent fare breakdown before booking, with clearer communication around peak-hour pricing, could help customers make informed decisions.

**2. Make availability more reliable**

If customers use Rapido for urgent travel, waiting for a captain can become a major pain point.

Understanding booking patterns across locations and peak hours could help improve captain availability and reduce waiting times.

**3. Make safety part of the experience**

Safety isn't just an operational concern; it is an important part of customer trust.

Better visibility into captain verification, helmet compliance and safety support could make customers feel more confident while booking.

**4. Reduce friction in the booking experience**

A booking journey should feel effortless.

The study highlights the need to improve app performance, minimise technical glitches and simplify the booking process. Even small improvements here can make a difference to repeat usage.

**5. Close the customer feedback loop**

Billing disputes, cancellations and service inconsistencies can quickly affect customer trust.

A faster complaint-resolution system and better communication can turn a frustrating experience into an opportunity to retain a customer.

These are product opportunities suggested by the research, not claims about features already implemented by Rapido.

## Beyond dashboards: Leadership getting on the ground

There is another side to understanding customers that I find equally interesting: getting out of the boardroom and experiencing the product firsthand.

Data can tell us how many customers cancelled a ride, how long they waited or how often they used the app. But it cannot always explain the frustration behind those numbers.

That is where direct customer interaction matters.

Rapido's leadership going on-ground, taking rides and interacting with customers offers an opportunity to understand the experience beyond dashboards and reports.

It is a reminder that customer research doesn't always need to begin with a survey. Sometimes, the best way to understand a product is to become its customer.

## My takeaway

One thing this study reinforces for me is that product improvement starts with curiosity.

A customer choosing UPI, cancelling a booking, waiting for a captain or preferring Rapido for an emergency journey is sharing a piece of information about their expectations.

The job of a product team is to connect these observations, identify patterns and turn them into meaningful improvements.

*Good products aren't built just by looking at what customers do. They're built by understanding why they do it.*
